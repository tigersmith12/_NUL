What is—and is not—emergent?

The honest boundary

NUL is a non-LLM artificial-life simulation. It uses a seeded random generator, hand-written rules, vector similarity, episodic memory, and a symbolic grammar. It does not call an AI service, load a model, or use training data.

“Nothing” means no predefined semantic ontology: no object names, taxonomy, dictionary, labelled concepts, or final task. It cannot mean no implementation. A process without any mechanism would not run.

The minimal bootstrap

  • The world emits recurrent, unlabelled four-number patterns. Some patterns affect energy when contacted.
  • The agent groups similar sensations into prototypes and generates a unique glyph and phonetic symbol for each cluster.
  • Contact outcomes alter the valence attached to its own symbols. Those associations influence which pattern it attends to next.
  • Sequential memories become candidate regularities. Repeated transitions increase confidence; these are fallible beliefs, not necessarily true laws.
  • A built-in combine operator lets the agent join two of its concepts. It invents the artifact’s symbol and name; the engine merely executes the operator.

Language

The green transcript is the agent’s local symbol stream. The gray text is an observer translation supplied by the program. Roman-letter names are a display convenience; the underlying concepts are numerical prototypes. Generated glyphs are the agent’s more native notation.

What drives it?

Energy regulation, novelty-weighted attention, similarity clustering, and action selection are engineered. The particular concepts, names, preferences, inferred relationships, artifacts, and sequence of intents are produced during the run. There is no hidden list of objects waiting to be unlocked.

Persistence

The complete world autosaves to browser-local storage every 120 ticks, on pause, and when the page closes. Saves provides manual restoration and portable JSON snapshots. Snapshots include the PRNG position and active world state, so imported runs continue deterministically. Ontology exports are readable summaries; world snapshots are resumable saves.

A note on early AI

Logic Theorist (1956), General Problem Solver (1959), and ELIZA (1966) demonstrated remarkable symbolic methods in constrained settings. They generally manipulated representations supplied by their authors; they did not autonomously define an open-ended world from raw experience. NUL borrows the transparent, local, symbolic spirit without claiming to reproduce those systems.

This is an experiment in representation and agency, not evidence of consciousness, understanding, or general intelligence. Export the ontology to inspect the actual state rather than trusting the narrative alone.
